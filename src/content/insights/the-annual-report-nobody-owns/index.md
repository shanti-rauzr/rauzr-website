Every supplement and amendment has a clear owner. The annual report can be different. It's due every year, it draws on QA, QC, production, labelling and supply chain, and it can end up with whoever has the lightest month.

That matters. For an ANDA, the annual report is where minor changes made during the year are reported for the first time. For a Type II DMF, it's how FDA is assured the file is still current. Here is what each report needs, and where teams get caught out.

## The ANDA annual report: what the rule says

Annual reports for NDAs are set out in 21 CFR 314.81(b)(2), and 21 CFR 314.98(b) applies the same reporting duty to every approved ANDA. [[1]](#ref-1) [[2]](#ref-2)

**When.** Each year, within 60 days of the anniversary date of US approval of the application. The report covers the annual reporting interval that ends on that anniversary date. [[1]](#ref-1)

**With which form.** Each annual report must be accompanied by a completed **Form FDA 2252** (Transmittal of Periodic Reports for Drugs for Human Use). [[1]](#ref-1) Not Form 356h, which goes with supplements and other ANDA submissions. FDA's eCTD team has stated it directly: annual report submissions must have a fillable 2252 form, and all other ANDA submissions must have a fillable 356h form. [[3]](#ref-3)

**What goes in, in this order.** [[1]](#ref-1)

1. **Summary** of significant new information that might affect safety, effectiveness or labelling, and the actions taken or planned.
2. **Distribution data** by NDC and strength, domestic and foreign.
3. **Labelling** currently in use, and a dated summary of labelling changes since the last report, or a statement that there were none.
4. **CMC changes**: a full description of the changes that didn't need a supplement, listed in the order they were implemented.
5. **Nonclinical** toxicology findings.
6. **Clinical data**, including pediatric data.
7. **Status of postmarketing study commitments.**
8. **Status of other postmarketing studies.**
9. **Log of outstanding regulatory business** (optional).

## Where the annual report goes wrong

The annual report is the reporting route for **minor changes only**. Under 21 CFR 314.70(d), these are changes with "minimal potential to have an adverse effect" on the product's identity, strength, quality, purity or potency. [[4]](#ref-4) A change with moderate potential needs a CBE-30 or CBE-0 supplement under 314.70(c). A change with substantial potential needs a prior approval supplement under 314.70(b), approved before product made with the change is distributed. ANDAs follow the same section through 21 CFR 314.97. [[4]](#ref-4)

For each minor change, the applicant has to give a statement that the effects of the change have been assessed, a full description including the site or area involved, the date it was implemented, and the data from the studies and tests used to assess it. [[4]](#ref-4) FDA's 2014 guidance on CMC changes for annual reports adds that each change should be described "in enough detail to allow the Agency to efficiently determine whether the appropriate reporting category has been used." [[5]](#ref-5)

That sentence is the heart of the problem. An annual report is often assembled near the deadline by going back through a year of change controls, and it may be the first time RA reads some of them closely. An equipment change at a second site, an updated test method, a revised in-process limit: each was assessed by QA at the time, but not always against the 314.70 categories.

When that review happens months later, three problems can appear:

- **A change that needed a supplement.** The change falls under 314.70(b) or (c), not (d), and product may already have been distributed with it. The fix is now a supplement and an investigation, not a line in the report.
- **Missing dates or data.** The change control is closed, but the implementation date or supporting data are hard to find.
- **Labelling drift.** The labelling in use differs from the last approved labelling, and no listed change explains it.

The annual report doesn't cause these problems. It's where they become visible.

## The DMF annual report: a separate duty

**Where the duty comes from.** It isn't in 21 CFR 314.81. It comes from FDA's DMF guidance: the 1989 Guideline for Drug Master Files and the draft guidance Drug Master Files, Revision 1, October 2019. [[6]](#ref-6) [[7]](#ref-7)

**When.** On the anniversary date of the original DMF submission. [[6]](#ref-6)

**With which form.** A new Form FDA 3938, with "Annual Report" selected as the submission type. FDA's instructions ask for a new 3938 with each submission. [[8]](#ref-8)

**What goes in.** According to the 2019 draft guidance, the Form 3938 instructions and FDA's annual report template: [[7]](#ref-7) [[8]](#ref-8) [[9]](#ref-9)

- a cover letter and a **statement of commitment signed by the DMF holder** that the DMF is current and the holder will comply with it. An agent filing for the holder should include the holder-signed statement with the report, not refer to eCTD section 1.2;
- a statement that the administrative information is current;
- the **dates of any amendments** reporting changes since the last annual report, or since the original filing;
- the **list of authorised parties**, and the list of parties whose **authorisation has been withdrawn**, with the dates of withdrawal.

**What it can't do.** The annual report "is not to be used for purposes of reporting new changes to a DMF." [[8]](#ref-8) Changes go in an amendment, under a separate eCTD sequence number. [[7]](#ref-7)

**Why it matters.** The annual report is required to keep a DMF "active". [[8]](#ref-8) Failure to submit it may lead to closure, and a closed DMF can't be reviewed in support of a new or amended application. [[7]](#ref-7)

**A related but separate duty.** Under 21 CFR 314.420(c), a DMF holder who adds, changes or deletes information in the DMF must notify, in writing, each person authorised to reference that information. [[10]](#ref-10) The 2019 draft guidance asks holders to do this well before making the change, with enough information for each customer to decide how to report it. [[7]](#ref-7) The annual report doesn't replace that notice.

## Two reports, one chain

When an API holder changes its process, each ANDA customer has to decide how to report it under its own application. If the notice is late or thin, the customer may only find the change while building its annual report, months after the decision was due.

## A short checklist for this year

**For each ANDA**

- Confirm the US approval anniversary and the 60-day window. Put both on the RA calendar with a named owner.
- Prepare Form FDA 2252, not 356h.
- Pull every change implemented in the reporting interval. For each, record the 314.70 category and who decided it.
- Treat any change that looks like CBE-30, CBE-0 or PAS as a separate issue now, not as an annual report line.
- For each minor change, check you have the implementation date, the site, the assessment statement and the supporting data.
- Compare the labelling in use with the last approved labelling, and explain every difference.

**For each Type II DMF**

- Confirm the anniversary of the original submission.
- Make sure the statement of commitment is signed by the holder. An agent shouldn't sign it or point to eCTD section 1.2.
- List the dates of every amendment since the last annual report.
- Reconcile the authorised-party list with every LoA issued and withdrawn, with withdrawal dates.
- Make sure no new change is described in the report. File it as an amendment.
- Check that each customer was told about each change in writing, ideally well before it was made.
